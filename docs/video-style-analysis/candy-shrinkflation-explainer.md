# Style Breakdown: "Why a ₹1 Candy Still Costs ₹1" (Indian Finance Academy)

Source: `Video-48965.mp4`. Vertical 720×1280 at 30 fps, H.264. Runtime 84.9 s (about 82 s of content plus a 2.5 s logo outro). Stereo AAC at 48 kHz.

---

## 1. Format and Structure

| Beat | Time | What happens | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:04 | "Ever wondered why a candy that cost ₹1 when you were a child…" | A nostalgia question that puts the viewer inside it |
| Time jump | 0:04–0:05 | Calendar pages fly from 2001 → 2005 → 2018 → 2026 | Turns "decades passed" into one image |
| Contrast | 0:05–0:09 | Coins stack up next to tea and milk: "…while almost everything else became more expensive?" | Sets up the paradox |
| Promise | 0:10–0:12 | A large stopwatch drops in: "We'll answer that in just 70 seconds." | Retention contract |
| Running timer | 0:12–1:22 | A small stopwatch in the top-left counts down 69 → 1 | Shows how much is left, so viewers stay to the end |
| Problem 1 | 0:12–0:28 | Brass scale: a 10% cost rise makes ₹1.10, which needs a 10-paise coin "that is barely used" | Logic, shown on a physical object |
| Problem 2 | 0:29–0:48 | Factory → sticker gun: the ₹1 sticker becomes ₹2, and doubling "can kill the very reason the candy sells" (one coin, one candy) | Raises the stakes |
| Answer | 0:49–1:03 | A press squeezes the candy, then a **SHRINKFLATION** title card and a biscuit pack shrinking 100 g → 75 g | The reveal, with the key term named |
| Mechanics | 1:04–1:15 | Recipe valves (milk/sugar), filling 4.8 g → 4.4 g, lighter wrapper, tighter transport crates | Quick list of the methods |
| Close | 1:16–1:22 | Back to the opening shop shot: "preserving the magical price tag of 1 Rupee." | Ends where it began |
| Outro | 1:22–1:25 | The scene dims and the IFA shield logo fades in | Branding |

**Narrative pattern:** Question → Paradox → Time-boxed promise → Two failed options → Named concept → How it works → Callback. The video names the one key term at its turning point and shows it as a full-screen title.

## 2. Editing Style

- **Almost no hard cuts.** Scene-change detection finds only 2 cuts in 85 s (at 4.3 s and 4.5 s, both inside the calendar transition). The video plays like **one continuous virtual camera** moving through a single 2.5D world (shop → counter → factory → shop).
- **Transitions are camera moves, not cuts:**
  - *Whip-pan with motion blur* (shop → factory at 0:28–0:29 and 0:48–0:49), with heavy directional blur for 3–6 frames.
  - *Push-in / pull-out* on the counter (the scale close-up at 0:22–0:27, then a pull back out).
  - *Rack focus / depth-of-field blur* (0:32: the jar goes soft as the sugar sacks come in).
  - *Object-driven transitions*: the calendar flips fill the frame and hide the change of setting.
- **Pacing:** a new visual action roughly every **1–1.5 s**, locked to the narration. Each spoken idea gets one visual, and nothing on screen sits still for more than about 2 s.
- **Bookending:** the first and last shots share the same composition (shop, two jars, coin, candy), so the video loops cleanly.
- **The hand as a "cursor":** a flat illustrated hand (red/blue thread bracelet, later a suit cuff and a watch) taps, slides, points and turns things. It drives every cause-and-effect beat and stands in for the viewer.

## 3. Motion Graphics and Illustration Style

- **Look:** flat-vector 2.5D illustration with soft gradients and light shadows. Not paper-cutout, not 3D. The objects are simple but have volume: glass jars with highlights, metallic coins, brass scales.
- **Camera:** a perspective counter plane with parallax between layers (shelves in the background, jars in the middle, coins and hand in front). The background shelves stay dim and slightly blurred, so the foreground objects stand out.
- **Palette:**
  - Background: deep navy and indigo (shop shelves, factory walls).
  - Hero colours: **orange/red candy wrappers**, **red** jar lid, **yellow** jar lid, **brass gold** scale.
  - Accents: **magenta/pink** price-starburst stickers, **mint/teal** for the SHRINKFLATION title and the digital scale readouts.
  - Warm tungsten light in the shop, cool blue in the factory. The colour temperature tells you where you are.
- **Recurring props as data:** the calendar (year), the stopwatch (time left), coins (money), the scale (comparison), digital scales (100 g → 75 g, 4.8 g → 4.4 g). Every number is **shown on an object**, never as a plain text overlay.
- **Motion feel:** springy ease-out/overshoot pops (price stickers, stopwatch drop), smooth eased tilts on the scale, mechanical linear motion in the factory (conveyors, presses, valves). Shapes do not squash and stretch much. It feels physical rather than cartoony.
- **UI feedback cues:** a teal ripple ring when a coin is tapped (0:43), a "−0.4 g" delta label beside the readout, and a "+10%" weight tag dropping onto the sugar, milk and candy.
- **Branding:** a small semi-transparent "INDIAN FINANCE ACADEMY" watermark in the top-right throughout, and the full logo only in the outro.

## 4. Captions and Typography

- **One line, 2–5 words**, centred in the lower third (about 85% down the frame). No background box. Bold, rounded, geometric sans-serif (Poppins/Montserrat-ExtraBold style).
- **Word-by-word karaoke highlight:** the base text is white and the **active or key word turns yellow-gold** (#F5C518-ish). Numbers and money ("₹1.10", "₹2", "10%") always get the highlight.
- Captions dim to grey just before a phrase changes (visible at 0:02 and 0:13). The phrase **fades out, then the next one snaps in**, with no slide or bounce.
- The only large on-screen text is the concept word **SHRINKFLATION**: teal, all caps, heavy, sitting in the scene.

## 5. Audio Style

Measured (ffmpeg `ebur128`, `volumedetect` and a spectrogram):

| Metric | Value | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Integrated loudness | **−14.1 LUFS** | Mastered to the Instagram/YouTube target |
| Loudness range | **2.8 LU** | Heavily compressed, so level barely changes |
| True peak | about −0.2 dBFS | Limited right up to the ceiling |
| Silences (< −35 dB, ≥ 0.25 s) | **none** | The bed never stops; there is no dead air |
| Side (L−R) level | −28 dB vs −17 dB mean | The voice sits in the centre; music and SFX add stereo width |

- **Voice-over:** a single narrator with a calm, conversational, explainer tone. The delivery is fast (about 2.5–3 words per second: about 200 words in 80 s) with dramatic pauses on "…or" and "terrible". The narration is mixed well above everything else.
- **Music bed:** a low, unobtrusive lo-fi/corporate-curious loop under the voice the whole way, with no big drops. It fades with the logo at the end.
- **SFX:** the spectrogram shows dense short transients and band-limited bursts (energy past 16 kHz) on the visual actions: coin clinks, calendar whooshes, stopwatch ticks, sticker pops, machine clunks, valve squeaks and conveyor hum. **Almost every movement on screen has a sound.**
- **Mix strategy:** voice first, then SFX for punctuation, then music as glue. It is compressed hard so it stays clear on phone speakers.

## 6. Why It Works (Takeaways)

1. **A countdown timer as a retention device:** the hook promises "70 seconds" and a stopwatch on screen shows it being kept.
2. **One continuous world** instead of cuts: the viewer is never "reset", which feels premium and keeps attention.
3. **Every abstract number becomes a physical object** (scale, coin, sticker, digital scale).
4. **Nostalgia hook plus a paradox question** in the first 4 s.
5. **The thesis is withheld** until about 60% of the way through, then delivered as a title card with the named concept.
6. **Bookended loop:** the ending matches the opening, which encourages rewatches.
